# Model-budget tiers

Use these tiers to decide how much model work to spend on a teaching task. They control research breadth, the amount of material developed, and review rounds. They are workflow presets, not subscription levels, model names, or promises about token charges.

Accept `精简` / `lite`, `标准` / `standard`, and `深入` / `deep`. Default to **standard** when none is specified. Record the choice in the teaching brief before drafting. Learner proficiency and available study time are separate fields: a small model budget does not imply an advanced reader or a short attention span.

## Choose the work that fits

| Tier | Teaching scope | Research and examples | Complete review rounds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lite | One explicitly bounded learning outcome and the prerequisite path needed to reach it | Read the essential source passages; develop one complete worked case, a purposeful visual when it clarifies the idea, and an independent check with its answer | 1 |
| Standard | A coherent introductory tutorial or the requested chapter sequence | Cover the main prerequisites and method, compare the nearest useful alternative, and develop guided practice followed by a transfer problem | 2 |
| Deep | A broader field entry or a demanding paper tutorial within the agreed scope | Compare several relevant approaches, investigate conflicting accounts and failure cases, and connect mathematical, visual, empirical, or implementation perspectives where useful | 3 |

The round counts are defaults for these presets. Each round includes production, review, correction, and rechecking as defined in [the tutorial loop](tutorial-loop.md). More rounds do not prove that a learner understands more.

## Keep a complete learning unit in every tier

Even in lite, establish the problem and objects, explain the indispensable foundations, show how to use the central idea, provide a question the reader can attempt independently, and supply a reasoned answer. Keep the assumptions and source support that make the argument valid. Inspect the actual deliverable and correct known blocking defects.

Save work by narrowing the target, removing optional branches, reusing verified sources and a working template, choosing a static diagram over a new interactive component when it teaches the same relationship, and editing the affected section rather than repeatedly rewriting the whole book. Do not save work by hiding missing prerequisites, omitting necessary qualifications, supplying an answerless problem, or skipping a readability check.

Use figures from a paper when they serve the selected learning outcome. Inspect the relevant source assets when an original figure is needed; do not download the source archive for every citation just to make the bibliography look complete. HTML and LaTeX retain the same visual and language standards in all tiers.

## Resolve scope and budget together

Treat a tier as a preference for work allocation unless the user also supplies a hard limit. Explicit content and format requirements remain requirements. If a requested whole-field treatment will not fit lite, propose a sequence of complete units and state what the current unit covers. Do not silently delete required topics or label a first unit as the completed whole course.

If the user requires all topics in the current delivery, explain the trade-off and retain that scope. If an explicit hard cap makes it impossible, ask one consequential scope question when interaction is allowed. In a no-question run, produce the most useful complete unit within the cap and identify the unmet portion explicitly. It remains incomplete relative to the original larger request.

Never infer deep from GPT Pro or another paid product. The Pro exception only skips the opening interview. A user can request Pro with lite, or another environment with deep. Do not switch models or upgrade a tier silently.

## Spend the budget where the reader needs it

Protect the hardest prerequisite and the final independent check. Allocate the remaining effort to optional comparisons, additional examples, deeper source investigation, and polish. Reserve capacity for final review and packaging instead of using the entire budget on reconnaissance or an elaborate introduction.

If usage is measurable, track it using the environment's actual measurements. If it is not, track concrete work performed and remaining scope. Do not invent token totals, percentage savings, or account limits. An explicit user cap overrides these relative presets.

When a source cannot be reached, try an appropriate alternative within the budget. If an essential claim remains unsupported, narrow that claim or record it as unresolved. A higher tier provides more opportunity to investigate; it does not convert missing evidence into evidence.

## Continue without starting over

When moving from lite to standard or deep, retain the accepted learning unit, source locations, notation, examples, and real learner attempts. Add the deferred branches rather than regenerating everything. For work across sessions, use [learning progress](../templates/learning-progress.md) to preserve what was delivered, what the learner actually demonstrated, and the next unresolved dependency. Do not infer learner mastery from the fact that material was generated.
